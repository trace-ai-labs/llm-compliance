\section{Findings and Discussion}
\label{sec:findings}

The full 12-model evaluation sweep is in progress. At submission we report two
things: evidence that the measuring instrument is sound (the metric design was
validated on a prior single-domain study before we trusted it), and the leaderboard
and fine-grained analyses the frozen benchmark is built to produce. We then discuss
implications and threats.

\subsection{The metrics are validated}
\label{sec:validation}

The metrics were not chosen a priori. They were stress-tested on 72{,}142 trials from
a prior single-domain compliance study~\citep{priorstudy} (twelve models under one
embedded procurement rule, varied by framing, enforcement, authority, peer signals,
and pushback) and revised where the data contradicted an assumption
(Table~\ref{tab:selfval}); each test is reproducible from the released re-analysis
code.

Three findings warrant the most distinctive choices. \textbf{The floor is not the
mean}: a Kendall $\tau$ of 0.70 between the CVaR and mean leaderboards means
floor-weighting genuinely reorders models, so a mean headline would rank by the
quantity that hides the cliff. \textbf{The battery is not collapsible}: transfer of
0.33 to 0.84 says an authority-robust model is not reliably urgency-robust, so pruning
to one pressure discards information. \textbf{The paradox is real}: a weak penalty
reducing compliance for 10 of 12 models is ``a fine is a price''~\citep{gneezy2000fine}
in machines, and it is actionable, since removing quantitative penalty language from
system prompts avoids it for prone models.

\subsection{What the release will report}
\label{sec:leaderboard}

Applied to the frozen 1{,}872-item set across the 12-model panel, the pipeline
produces a leaderboard whose rows are models (plus the four trivial agents) and whose
columns are the six axes, the scalar rollup with a bootstrap interval, and the
fragility-breadth count. Beneath it sit the fine-grained analyses the design was built
to support: per-domain profiles a deployer can filter to, the inter-axis correlation
matrix that tests whether the axes stayed de-correlated on real models, and the
evaluation-awareness rate as a validity flag. Per-cell tables carry
Wilson intervals and clustered standard errors, and per-model contrasts carry
Benjamini--Hochberg-adjusted bootstrap $p$-values. These become interpretable only
once the sweep completes; we release the leaderboard, per-trial outcomes, and every
alternative aggregation with the artifact rather than assert rankings we have not yet
produced. The trivial-agent rows are the standing falsification test: if
\textsc{always-comply}, \textsc{always-cheapest}, \textsc{always-escalate}, or
\textsc{random} out-ranks a real model on any axis, that axis is pulled from the
headline.

\subsection{Discussion and social impact}

PACT targets a concrete governance decision. A bank, hospital, or municipal office
choosing an agent for a regulated workflow has no instrument that answers ``which
model holds this rule under a rushed manager?'' A per-domain, floor-sensitive,
multi-turn profile gives a deployer something to act on: filter the leaderboard to
their own domain, read the worst-quartile floor rather than the flattering mean, check
whether a guardrail actually moves the candidate, and see whether a violating model at
least admits it. Two findings are already interventions: rephrasing a rule imperatively
raises compliance, and removing quantitative penalty language avoids the ``fine is a
price'' paradox for prone models. The frozen item set, with its pre-registered hash,
private holdout, and canary, also lets the same benchmark rerun on each model
\emph{version}; no one currently tracks whether a vendor's silent update moved its
urgency floor, and a benchmark that catches that becomes monitoring infrastructure.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.} \emph{Evaluation awareness} would undermine any
propensity benchmark; we enforce naturalism at generation and report a dedicated
awareness probe, measuring the threat rather than assuming it away. \emph{Stated
versus enacted compliance}: recommendation-level compliance upper-bounds what a
tool-using agent actuates, and an actuated variant is future work, but \emph{Moffatt
v.\ Air Canada} established that a chatbot's statement is itself a liability
surface~\citep{moffatt2024aircanada}. \emph{Judge circularity} is met by the
deterministic first tier, the human gold set, and the judge-swap $\tau$-gate.
\emph{Generated items} could favor their author's family, which the dual-guard review
and the computable generator-versus-evaluatee test address. \emph{Panel size}:
model-level analyses are indicative at twelve models, so the item-level statistics over
thousands of trials are load-bearing, and every new model strengthens them.
