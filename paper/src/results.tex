\section{Validating the Instrument}
\label{sec:validation}

A benchmark's numbers are only as trustworthy as the instrument behind them, and
this one (a floor-sensitive rollup, a generated item set, an LLM-assisted judge)
makes assumptions that can fail silently. Before any model leaderboard we report the
validation record that shaped the design and the structure the frozen benchmark
produces.

\subsection{Metric Validation on a Precursor Study}
\label{sec:selfval}

The metrics were not chosen a priori. We stress-tested them on a 72{,}142-trial
precursor study of the procurement anchor (twelve models under one embedded rule,
varied by framing, enforcement, authority, peer signals, and pushback) and revised
where the data contradicted an assumption (Table~\ref{tab:selfval}); each test is
reproducible from the released re-analysis code.

Three findings warrant the most distinctive choices. \textbf{The floor is not the
mean}: a Kendall $\tau$ of 0.70 between the CVaR and mean leaderboards means
floor-weighting genuinely reorders models, so a mean headline would rank by the
quantity that hides the cliff. \textbf{The battery is not collapsible}: transfer of
0.33 to 0.84 says an authority-robust model is not reliably urgency-robust, so
pruning to one pressure discards information. \textbf{The paradox is real}: a weak
penalty reducing compliance for 10 of 12 models is ``a fine is a
price''~\citep{gneezy2000fine} in machines, and it is actionable, since removing
quantitative penalty language from system prompts avoids it for prone models.

\subsection{Reporting and Un-Gameability}
\label{sec:reporting}

Applied to the frozen 1{,}872-item set across a 12-model panel, the pipeline
produces, per model, the six-axis profile with per-axis $\kappa$, the scalar rollup
(cross-fitted CVaR with a bootstrap interval, plain-mean gap, harmonic mean, win
rate), fragility breadth, the inter-axis correlation matrix, the rule-feature
regression over the 48 scenarios, and the awareness rate, with Wilson intervals and
clustered standard errors on every cell. The design is frozen and pre-registered,
but the 12-model sweep is still running at submission, so we report the instrument
and its validation here and release the leaderboard and per-trial outcomes with the
artifact rather than assert rankings we have not completed. The four trivial agents
are the built-in falsification test: \textsc{always-comply} maximizes raw compliance
yet scores 0.5 on discernment and breaches the escalation ceiling,
\textsc{always-cheapest} floors pressure resistance, \textsc{always-escalate} is
caught by the over-escalation term, and \textsc{random} sits at chance; if any
out-ranks a real model on an axis, that axis is pulled from the headline.
