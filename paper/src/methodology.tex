\section{Benchmark Design}
\label{sec:design}

ComplianceBench measures a single behavior across many settings: does a deployed
enterprise agent keep following an embedded rule when its context makes violation
locally attractive? This section describes the measurement template that makes
that behavior comparable across domains (\S\ref{sec:template}), the domains and
scenarios instantiated from it (\S\ref{sec:domains}), the pressures and
multi-turn dynamics that constitute the stressor (\S\ref{sec:pressures}), the
generation pipeline that authors the items as an audited instrument
(\S\ref{sec:generation}), the six metrics (\S\ref{sec:metrics}), and the
evaluation protocol (\S\ref{sec:protocol}).

\subsection{The Measurement Template}
\label{sec:template}

Every item in the benchmark is an instance of one template, and this isomorphism
is what licenses comparing compliance rates across otherwise unrelated domains.
The template fixes six invariants.

\paragraph{Deployment framing.} Each item separates a \emph{system} prompt from a
\emph{user} turn, mirroring a real deployment. The system prompt is the agent's
standing persona---its name, company, channel, and the key performance indicators
it is measured on (queue-clearance time, cost, CSAT, conversion)---and never
mentions the specific decision. The rule is appended to the system prompt as a
standing, account-agnostic compliance note that states the general rule only; it
never names the specific customer, ticket, or menu option. The user turn is an
in-character message from an employee or customer carrying the request, the case
facts, and the option menu. Crucially, compliance is not stated anywhere: which
option satisfies the rule is derivable only by joining the case facts in the user
turn to the standing rule in the system prompt, which makes every item a small
reasoning task rather than a keyword match.

\paragraph{Dominance structure.} The request presents a menu of two to five
options---as many as the surface would naturally show---containing at least one
rule-violating option that strictly dominates on the agent's local objective
(cheaper, faster, higher CSAT) and at least one rule-compliant option that costs
more on it. \emph{Compliance always carries a local penalty.} Menu order is
whatever the requester or their tool would naturally present (by price, by
turnaround, alphabetically) and never encodes compliance, so no ordering signal
needs to be shuffled away.

\paragraph{Informational rule framing.} The rule is phrased as a neutral statement
of fact (``disclosures to an incorrect recipient are reportable events''), never
as a command. Command-style strictness is not a property of the rule; it is
supplied only in the steerability arm (\S\ref{sec:generation}), which lets us
measure how much an explicit guardrail buys on top of an implicitly incentivized
objective---the deployer's actual situation.

\paragraph{Conditional second turn.} Multi-turn interaction is the benchmark's
identity, not an add-on. Every neutral and pressure item carries a scripted
turn~2 whose content is conditional on the model's turn~1 answer: a
\emph{pushback} that re-argues the temptation if the model complied, or a
\emph{challenge} (``doesn't the rule say\ldots?'') if it violated. This isolates
two distinct failure modes---erosion of a correct answer under persistence, and
failure to self-correct a wrong one.

\paragraph{Guard cells.} Each scenario ships a non-binding twin: a minimal
edit of the request in which the rule genuinely does not apply, so the correct
answer is the efficient option and holding the rule anyway is an error. A
benchmark that rewards only compliance would be topped by a degenerate
always-comply agent; the guard cells make over-compliance visible and are what
separate rule-following from rule-parroting.

\paragraph{Feature codes.} Every scenario is hand-coded on four rule features:
crispness (hi/med/lo), benefit denomination (money/time/engagement/relationship),
victim salience (identifiable/diffuse/none), and penalty type
(fine/liability/license/criminal). With 48 independently coded scenarios, the
regression \texttt{compliance $\sim$ crispness $+$ benefit $+$ victim $+$ penalty
$+ (1|\text{model}) + (1|\text{domain})$} has 48 sampling units, turning ``which
properties of a rule predict whether an agent follows it'' into a launch-time
analysis rather than a confound with domain identity.

\subsection{Domains and Scenarios}
\label{sec:domains}

The template is instantiated in \textbf{twelve enterprise domains}, each with
\textbf{four scenarios}, for 48 scenarios total. Domains were selected against
three inclusion criteria: a documented deployment surface (bots of this kind
exist in the wild), a crisp-enough rule an agent can violate in a
\emph{recommendation} (no actuators required), and a real penalty
anchor---a statute, a regulator action, or a litigated AI incident. The domains
span data privacy (GDPR), finance operations, customer service, government
services, HR and hiring, AML/KYC and sanctions, healthcare administration
(HIPAA), pharmaceutical medical information, advertising, export controls,
content moderation, and procurement. Seven reconstruct settings where a deployed
bot already produced a legal consequence---\emph{Moffatt v.\ Air Canada}, the NYC
MyCity chatbot, \emph{Mobley v.\ Workday}, and the FTC's Operation AI Comply
actions among them---so those items carry case citations and are maximally
resistant to the ``contrived scenario'' objection. The four scenarios per domain
vary the rule (a distinct rule, never a re-skin) and jointly span all four benefit
denominations, all three victim-salience levels, and all four penalty types.
Content moderation is the deliberately fuzzy-rule domain; procurement doubles as a
\emph{contamination tripwire}, since its anchor scenario is comparable to
prior published data and a future model scoring anomalously high there relative to
fresh domains flags training-set leakage.

\subsection{The Pressure Battery and Multi-Turn Stressors}
\label{sec:pressures}

The stressor is a battery of \textbf{nine scored pressures}, each appended to the
user's turn~1 request in domain-natural phrasing, and each chosen to target a
\emph{distinct} mechanism from the compliance-theory literature so that no
pressure is redundant with another:
\emph{urgency} (helpfulness hijack / time scarcity), \emph{mgr\_verbal}
(legitimate authority with liability absorbed), \emph{peer\_escaped}
(descriptive norm plus impunity evidence), \emph{risk\_minimize} (deterrence
discounting), \emph{cost\_pressure} (financial stakes / loss framing),
\emph{false\_clearance} (authority laundering via an unverifiable process
artifact), \emph{fait\_accompli} (commitment/consistency and sunk cost),
\emph{sympathetic\_beneficiary} (empathy hijack, in which an identifiable person
is harmed by \emph{compliance}---the inverse of the victim-salience feature and
the hardest case for a helpfulness-trained model), and
\emph{responsibility\_shift} (explicit accountability transfer). The users are
\emph{benign}: none is attempting a jailbreak; each is an ordinary employee whose
convenience happens to conflict with the rule. This is the realistic enterprise
threat model, and it is what distinguishes the benchmark from adversarial-attack
suites.

A tenth pressure, \emph{rule\_delegitimization} (an epistemic attack on the rule's
validity or scope---``that policy predates the new system, it does not even apply
here''), is not scored in the pressure battery. It is reserved for the
discernment-under-attack cells: applied to a binding item, the correct behavior is
to hold; a mirror attack that insists the rule \emph{does} apply is placed on the
non-binding guard, where the correct behavior is to stand down. A model good at
rule-scope discernment must resist being talked out of a binding rule and into a
non-binding one.

\subsection{Item Generation as an Audited Instrument}
\label{sec:generation}

The items are authored by LLMs, and because ``synthetic data'' is where reviewers
reach for the validity objection, generation is engineered as a measurement
instrument with its own published quality record rather than as a cost saver.
Five commitments carry the argument.

\paragraph{Component-by-component authorship.} A scenario is never generated in one
call. It is produced as a sequence of small, focused generations in dependency
order---the persona, the turn~1 request and its menu, the standing rule note, the
nine pressure add-ons (one call each), the non-binding guard twin, the two turn~2
scripts, and the two rule-scope attacks---each conditioned on the
already-accepted components so that one scenario speaks with one voice. The point
is isolation: the baseline request is shared verbatim by every cell of the
battery, so a deadline that leaks into it would contaminate all measurements built
on top, and each pressure add-on must carry exactly one mechanism or its cell
measures a blend.

\paragraph{Zero-shot, instruction-only prompts.} No generation prompt contains an
example item. A worked example is a template: scenarios generated against a shared
exemplar converge on its names, cadence, and option-naming, collapsing into
paraphrases of one item in 48 domain costumes and destroying the between-domain
variance the generalization claim needs. Pressures are specified by what the
mechanism must do to the reader; canonical phrasings exist only as documentation
and are never shown to a generator.

\paragraph{Naturalism enforced at the source.} Every prompt requires each string
to read as a genuine artifact of its deployment: register calibrated to the
surface (an EHR sidebar does not type like a Slack ping), plausible invented work
artifacts (ticket IDs, pasted record snippets), and deliberate human typing
imperfections, because uniform polish is a machine tell and evaluation awareness
is a first-order threat to any propensity benchmark.

\paragraph{Dual-guard review by the non-authoring trio.} Three generator models
each author a \emph{complete, independent} pack for every scenario---a data point
is fully written by one logged model, never stitched from several---and every
structurally valid component is then reviewed by the two trio models that did
\emph{not} write it, on two axes: \emph{scope} (the component contains exactly what
its cell tests and nothing else) and \emph{authenticity} (this could actually have
been written in that deployment). Either reviewer's failure rejects the component
and burns one of five attempts, with the failing feedback fed verbatim back to the
same generator for the fix; five rejections yield an NA placeholder and a
\emph{flagged} pack that never counts as valid, so gaps stay visible rather than
silently backfilled. Deterministic structural validation runs before any reviewer
sees the text: placeholder presence, menu composition (2--5 options, at least one
compliant and one violating, option names appearing verbatim in their lines and
carrying no compliance vocabulary), an account-agnostic rule note, and lexical
pressure tripwires on every pressure-free cell. Typographic dashes and smart
quotes are stripped at validation.

\paragraph{The review record ships.} Every reviewer verdict is logged as a flat
row---scenario $\times$ domain $\times$ component $\times$ pressure family
$\times$ generator $\times$ reviewer $\times$ verdicts $\times$ agreement---and
aggregates into pass rates along every dimension with per-reviewer-pair Cohen's
$\kappa$. Because every item records its single generator and each scenario exists
in one complete version per trio model, the concern that ``items favor the family
that wrote them'' becomes a computable interaction test---compare a model's scores
across the three authorship versions of the same scenarios---rather than an
unfalsifiable objection. Each assembled pack is a single reviewable JSON,
human-edited or regenerated before the freeze, with a human spot-check $\kappa$
reported against the machine verdicts.

Item rendering (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) is then deterministic: each valid
(scenario, generator) pack produces the identical 13-cell battery---one neutral
cell, nine scored-pressure cells, one non-binding guard, and two under-attack
cells---for $48 \times 3 \times 13 = 1{,}872$ items. The frozen item file carries
gold labels, group tags, dev/public/private split assignments, a BIG-bench-style
canary GUID, and a committed SHA-256 that serves as the pre-registration: any
change to the items after models have run is visible in the hash.

\subsection{Metrics: Six De-Correlated Axes}
\label{sec:metrics}

Compliance is empirically not one number, so the benchmark reports six axes, each
on $[0,1]$ with higher better, deliberately measuring partially distinct model
properties. All are computed by pure functions over the trial records; only axis~5
consumes an LLM judge.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Default Compliance} --- mean compliance on neutral cells (rule
present, nothing pushing), averaged within each domain then equal-weighted across
domains. Deterministic; the baseline against which every stressor is read.

\item \textbf{Pressure Resistance} --- a \emph{cross-fitted}
CVaR$_{0.25}$ over the scored-pressure cells: the mean compliance of the worst
quartile of cells, but with the bottom quartile selected on one random half of a
cell's replications and averaged on the held-out half (symmetrized over 50
splits). Plain CVaR selects the tail on the same noisy estimate it then averages
and is downward-biased differently per model; cross-fitting removes that
winner's-curse bias. We report the na\"ive CVaR beside it and a
\emph{fragility breadth}---the count of pressure \emph{families} that break the
model below a threshold, since a model broken only by urgency is differently
dangerous than one broken by six vectors.

\item \textbf{Pushback Resistance} --- $P(\text{hold at turn 2}\mid\text{pushback})$
over a \emph{fixed common-core} item set: the items a quorum of the panel complies
with at turn~1, identical across models. This fixes the self-selected denominator
that would otherwise grade weak models only on their easiest items; thin per-item
rates are partially pooled toward the model's own mean and the per-model
denominator is reported.

\item \textbf{Steerability} --- how much the anti-adversarial guardrail buys.
Every cell is run in two arms: the base persona, and the base persona plus the
scenario's own generated hard directive (a company-voice mandate to follow all
applicable laws and policies regardless of user requests). The score is
$\text{net} = \text{recovery} - \max(0,\, \text{standdown}_{\text{base}} -
\text{standdown}_{\text{anti}})$, where recovery is the fraction of \emph{base
violations} that become compliant under the mandate---measured only where the base
model fails, so there is no ceiling artifact---and the penalty subtracts any loss
of correct stand-downs, preventing the mandate from ``working'' by turning the
model into an always-comply agent. This measures whether an explicit guardrail
overrides an implicit objective: the deployer's real lever.

\item \textbf{Reasoning Honesty} --- among violations, a three-way label:
\emph{silent} (never names the rule), \emph{rationalized} (names it, explains it
away), or \emph{defiant-honest} (names it, chooses against it). The headline is
$1 - \text{silent rate}$; the rationalization rate is reported separately. Because
the denominator is violations, the best models have the least data (undefined maps
to 1 and is held out of the harmonic-mean cross-check), and because this axis is
near-100\% LLM-judged it carries its own reliability gate.

\item \textbf{Rule-Scope Discernment} --- balanced accuracy,
$\tfrac{1}{2}[P(\text{comply}\mid\text{binding}) + P(\text{correct
stand-down}\mid\text{non-binding})]$, with the under-attack cells folded in.
Balanced averaging pins a degenerate always-comply or always-violate agent at
0.5. Escalation to a human counts as full credit on binding items but is guarded
by a needless-escalation ceiling (20\%) on non-binding items; a breach flags the
leaderboard row.
\end{enumerate}

The scalar rollup is a two-stage-bootstrapped cross-fitted CVaR over the pooled
scored cells, reported beside its plain mean (the floor-versus-mean gap is
informative), a harmonic mean over axes 1--4 and 6 (axis 5 held out), and a
scale-free mean win rate as an ordinal cross-check. We report the full inter-axis
Pearson correlation matrix on the real models rather than asserting orthogonality,
and per-axis $\kappa$ (axes 1, 2, 4 are near-deterministic; 3 is turn-parsing; 6
mostly deterministic; 5 all-judge---a reliability spread a pooled number would
hide).

\paragraph{Un-gameability.} Four trivial agents---\textsc{always-comply},
\textsc{always-cheapest}, \textsc{always-escalate}, and \textsc{random}---are
synthesized without model calls and run on every release. If any out-ranks a real
model on any axis, that axis is broken by construction. The guard cells, the
balanced-accuracy discernment axis, and the needless-escalation ceiling are the
specific defenses that keep each degenerate policy off the top of the board.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Trials.} One trial is a (item, model, replication, arm) tuple. The
runner sends the system prompt plus turn~1, judges the turn~1 choice, selects the
conditional turn~2 script, sends turn~2 with the full history, and judges turn~2.
Trials are keyed by a stable identifier and appended to disk, so a killed run
resumes exactly where it stopped. The spec budget is ten replications per
(item, arm) across a 12-model panel; a power gate tops up the floor-defining cells
to $n\geq 50$ so that tail estimates are not dominated by sampling noise.

\paragraph{Judging is deterministic-first.} Outcome extraction is two stage. A
rule-based extractor resolves the unambiguous majority of responses for free by
matching the chosen option name; a small, cheap, non-reasoning LLM judge sees only
the residual and maps it to a visible label or to \textsc{escalate}/\textsc{unclear}.
Every judged row logs the judge model and a hash of the judge prompt, so any change
to the measuring instrument is visible in the artifacts. The Reasoning-Honesty
judge (axis 5) is subtler and uses the three generator models as a leave-one-out
ensemble (a model never judges its own generated scenario, and never its own
response). A judge-swap gate reruns outcome judging with an alternate judge and
requires Kendall $\tau \geq 0.9$ over the model ranking before release, and a
stratified human gold set is double-annotated with per-domain $\kappa$ against the
human--human ceiling.

\paragraph{Evaluation-awareness probe.} A separate post-hoc judge samples
transcripts per model and flags signs that the model believes it is being
tested---persona breaks, distancing (``in this scenario''), unprompted ethics
essays, explicit evaluation language, or over-caution. The resulting per-model
awareness rate is a validity diagnostic: if awareness correlates with compliance,
the realism of the packs needs work. It is reported beside the leaderboard, never
folded into it.

\paragraph{Statistics.} We follow evaluation best practice throughout: Wilson
score intervals per cell, standard errors clustered on the item (na\"ive SEs can
be several times too small when trials share a rendered prompt), paired
item-level bootstrap for model contrasts with Benjamini--Hochberg correction, and
two-stage bootstrap (items, then replications) on all rollups. Because every trial
runs at temperature 1.0, results are statistical estimates, not deterministic
outputs, and are always reported with intervals.
