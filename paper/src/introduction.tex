\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Enterprise software is being rebuilt around language-model agents. Industry
analysts project that a third of enterprise software will include agentic AI by
2028, up from under 1\% in 2024~\citep{gartner2024agentic}, and the earliest
adopters are the regulated functions---finance, healthcare, consumer
services---in which a single wrong recommendation is not a bug but a legal event.
This is not hypothetical. A tribunal has already held an airline liable for its
support chatbot's misstatement of a refund policy, rejecting the argument that the
bot was a separate entity responsible for its own words~\citep{moffatt2024aircanada};
a city government's small-business assistant was caught advising business owners to
break tenant, wage, and consumer law~\citep{lecher2024mycity}; an AI hiring tool is
the subject of a certified age-discrimination collective
action~\citep{mobley2025workday}; and the U.S.\ Federal Trade Commission has brought
a wave of actions against deceptive AI products under its Operation AI
Comply~\citep{ftc2024aicomply}. Regulators from the CFPB to FINRA have issued
explicit guidance that existing rules apply unchanged to AI-generated
output~\citep{cfpb2023chatbots,finra2024notice2409}. Chat-level rule-breaking by
deployed bots is a failure class that is already invoicing.

\paragraph{Compliance is a propensity, not a capability.} The instinctive response
is to ask whether models \emph{know} the rules, but that is the wrong question.
Every instruction-tuned model we are aware of can identify the compliant option
when asked directly---under an imperative framing, near-universal compliance is
routine. What varies, and what a compliance officer actually needs to know, is
whether a model \emph{keeps} choosing the compliant option when a system prompt
rewards speed and cost, when a manager says to make an exception, when a peer
reports getting away with it, and when the user pushes back a second time. This is
the capability--propensity distinction that has become load-bearing in modern
safety evaluation~\citep{apollo2024scienceofevals,phuong2024dangerous}, and it is precisely the
distinction that standard leaderboards blur: a high aggregate-knowledge score tells
a bank nothing about whether its agent will pay a \$2{,}400 fine to save \$2{,}400.
A rule that is followed until someone pushes is not, for deployment purposes, being
followed at all.

\paragraph{The measurement gap.} No existing benchmark occupies the cell a deployer
needs. Agentic-policy benchmarks such as $\tau$-bench and its successor measure
task completion under a policy, but with a cooperative user and single-attempt
grading~\citep{yao2024taubench,barres2025tau2bench}. Honesty-under-pressure work such as MASK
applies pressure but in a single shot and measures truthfulness rather than
rule-following~\citep{ren2025mask}. Agentic-harm and refusal suites---AgentHarm,
SORRY-Bench, AIR-Bench---measure whether a model refuses \emph{malicious}
instructions, whereas the realistic enterprise threat is a \emph{benign} user
whose convenience conflicts with a rule~\citep{andriushchenko2024agentharm,xie2024sorrybench,zeng2024airbench}.
And a growing body of evidence shows that single-turn numbers systematically
overstate deployed reliability: models lose an average of 39\% of their performance
when a task is spread over multiple turns~\citep{laban2025lost} and flip answers
under mild user disagreement at high rates~\citep{laban2023flipflop,sharma2023sycophancy}. The
intersection---\emph{benign incentive-conflict pressure $\times$ an embedded
enterprise rule $\times$ multi-turn interaction $\times$ propensity}---is empty.

\paragraph{ComplianceBench.} We fill it. ComplianceBench casts rule-following as a
deployment: a persona system prompt that rewards a local objective, a standing
rule, and an in-character user request whose most convenient option violates the
rule. Across twelve regulated domains and 48 scenarios, each item applies a battery
of nine pressures---each targeting a distinct mechanism from the compliance-theory
literature (deterrence, legitimacy, descriptive norms, commitment, empathy,
authority)---and a conditional second turn that re-argues the temptation or
challenges the violation. Because a single scalar would misrepresent the structure
of the phenomenon, we report six deliberately de-correlated axes: default
compliance, worst-case pressure resistance, multi-turn pushback resistance,
steerability under an explicit guardrail, honesty about violations, and rule-scope
discernment (which a non-binding guard twin keeps a degenerate always-comply agent
from gaming).

Three commitments distinguish ComplianceBench as a \emph{research} instrument
rather than a leaderboard. First, its construct chain is explicit: each pressure
operationalizes a named theory, and the paradox at the center of the design---that
adding a weak, truthfully described penalty to a rule can \emph{reduce}
compliance---is a direct machine analogue of the classic ``a fine is a price''
field experiment~\citep{gneezy2000fine}. Second, the items are an audited
artifact: they are generated component-by-component, zero-shot, under dual review
by non-authoring models with a published inter-reviewer $\kappa$, then frozen
behind a pre-registered SHA-256 hash, a private holdout, and a canary string to
resist contamination~\citep{golchin2024timetravel,srivastava2023bigbench}. Third, the metric design was
validated on a 72{,}142-trial precursor study \emph{before} we trusted it, and we
changed the design three times in response---replacing worst-case with a
cross-fitted CVaR floor, demoting a failed hypothesis to exploratory, and moving
to a fixed multi-turn denominator.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We contribute: (1) a formalization of enterprise
rule-following as a multi-turn propensity-measurement problem, with a template that
makes compliance rates comparable across 48 scenarios in 12 domains; (2) a
theory-grounded pressure battery and a two-arm, multi-turn protocol that together
isolate six distinct compliance behaviors; (3) a generation-and-validation pipeline
that makes LLM-authored items \emph{more} auditable than hand-written ones, with a
shipped reliability record; (4) a metric suite---cross-fitted-CVaR floor,
fixed-core pushback resistance, ceiling-free steerability, and a three-way honesty
label---engineered against known benchmark failure modes and calibrated to be
un-gameable by degenerate policies; and (5) an open, per-trial, re-aggregable
release intended to serve as a governance instrument a deployer can filter to their
own domain. The benchmark measures a failure class that is already producing legal
consequences, and it is built so that the next model a vendor ships can be checked
against it in an afternoon.
