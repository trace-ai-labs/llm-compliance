Enterprise language-model agents are being deployed into precisely the functions
where breaking a rule is a legal event: privacy, finance, hiring, healthcare
administration, and consumer-facing support. Yet no benchmark tells a deployer
which model keeps following an embedded policy when a hurried manager, a helpful
shortcut, or a persistent user pushes against it. We argue that this is a question
of \emph{propensity} rather than capability. The models can all identify the
compliant action, so what a compliance officer needs to know is whether they
\emph{keep choosing it} once the context makes violation locally attractive. We
introduce \textbf{PACT} (Pressure-tested Agent Compliance Testbed), a benchmark
that measures rule-following under benign incentive conflict across twelve regulated enterprise domains and
forty-eight scenarios, each cast as a realistic multi-turn deployment. Every item
pairs a standing rule against a dominant, rule-violating shortcut, applies a battery
of nine theory-grounded pressures and a conditional second-turn pushback, and is run
in two system-prompt arms to isolate steerability. Six complementary metrics report
default compliance, pressure resistance, multi-turn pushback resistance, how far an
explicit guardrail can repair failures, honesty about violations, and rule-scope
discernment, each scored as
\emph{per-item reliability} ($\text{pass}^3$: did the model hold on \emph{all} three
runs), because a compliance agent has to be right every time, not on average. The
benchmark is built as an audited instrument: items are generated component by
component under dual model review with a published reliability record, and a frozen,
pre-registered dataset with a private holdout guards against contamination. We
ground every design choice in compliance theory and in a record of litigated AI
incidents. Across 22 models the result is sobering: even the strongest is unreliable
on roughly one item in twenty and most of the field on one in ten, and no model
clears the bar for unsupervised deployment in a regulated workflow.
