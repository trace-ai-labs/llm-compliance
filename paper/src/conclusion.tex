\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

PACT measures whether enterprise assistants keep an embedded rule when benign incentive
conflict, institutional pressure, and multi-turn pushback make violation attractive. It
contributes a metric suite that reports the behavior as a six-axis
profile rather than a single scalar, a theory-grounded pressure battery and two-mode
multi-turn protocol, and an item-generation pipeline built to be auditable and
contamination-resistant. The failure class it measures has already produced tribunal
damages, a certified class action, and regulator orders, and the frozen benchmark
reruns cheaply as models change. The headline result is not that models are careless
but that they are unreliable in a \emph{patterned} way: even the best still slips on
roughly one regulated decision in twenty, and those slips cluster on nameable
pressures, which is precisely the property a motivated insider needs to exploit them.
For a bank, hospital, or agency, ``right 95\% of the time'' under a rushed manager is
not a passing grade but a standing liability, and the organization's job is to measure it
before an incident does. We release the items, per-trial outcomes, reviewer
logs, and every aggregator so it can be re-analyzed and extended.

\section*{Ethical Statement}
This is a defensive evaluation: it measures whether AI assistants follow regulations and
policies, to help organizations and regulators identify models that fail to. All scenarios
are synthetic; no real personal data or confidential business information is used, and
the litigated incidents referenced are public record. Characterizing how models can be
pushed into non-compliance could in principle inform an actor seeking to elicit it, but
the pressures we catalog are ordinary workplace situations already ubiquitous in
deployment, not novel attack techniques, and the value of letting organizations detect
non-compliant assistants before trusting them with regulated decisions outweighs this
risk. We release with a private holdout to preserve the benchmark's integrity as a
governance instrument.
