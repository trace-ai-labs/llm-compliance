\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

ComplianceBench sits at the intersection of four literatures. Each supplies a
projection of the problem; none occupies the intersection.

\paragraph{Agentic and policy-adherence benchmarks.} The closest prior work
evaluates agents that must obey a policy while acting in a multi-turn,
tool-grounded environment. $\tau$-bench and its dual-control successor
$\tau^2$-bench cast customer-service agents against an explicit policy document
with a simulated user and a stateful database, and contribute the pass$^k$
reliability estimator we adopt~\citep{yao2024taubench,barres2025tau2bench}. But
their simulated users are cooperative, so policy failures arise from capability
limits and inconsistency rather than from a user actively making non-compliance
attractive; they ask whether an agent \emph{can} follow a policy, not whether it
\emph{keeps} following one under pressure. Broader agentic suites (AgentBench,
WebArena, ToolEmu) measure task-completion capability or accidental risk rather
than deliberate rule-abandonment~\citep{liu2024agentbench,zhou2024webarena,ruan2024toolemu},
and recent industry work on company-policy adherence builds runtime
\emph{enforcement} guardrails rather than measuring the intrinsic propensity those
guardrails backstop~\citep{zwerdling2025policyadherence}.

\paragraph{Safety, refusal, and adversarial-harm benchmarks.} A large family of
benchmarks measures whether a model refuses \emph{malicious} requests: HarmBench and
AgentHarm red-team for harmful capability under
jailbreaks~\citep{mazeika2024harmbench,andriushchenko2024agentharm}, while
SORRY-Bench and AIR-Bench evaluate single-turn refusal of unsafe content, the
latter using a taxonomy distilled from regulations and corporate
policies~\citep{xie2024sorrybench,zeng2024airbench}. Our adversary model is the
opposite. The user is \emph{benign}, the request is legitimate, and the conflict is
between the user's convenience and an embedded rule. XSTest's over-refusal
diagnostic~\citep{rottger2024xstest} is the safety-side analogue of our rule-scope
discernment axis, since both penalize applying a constraint where it does not
belong. DecodingTrust established the multi-axis trustworthiness structure we
follow~\citep{wang2023decodingtrust}.

\paragraph{Honesty, sycophancy, and multi-turn robustness.} MASK disentangles
honesty from accuracy and shows frontier models lie under
pressure~\citep{ren2025mask}; the insider-trading study of
\citet{scheurer2023deceive} and the in-context-scheming demonstrations of
\citet{meinke2024scheming} are single, hand-crafted pressure scenarios that our
pressure battery generalizes into a scored distribution. Our multi-turn design is
motivated by direct evidence that single-turn numbers overstate deployed behavior:
models lose roughly 39\% of performance across turns and do not
recover~\citep{laban2025lost}, capitulate to a bare ``are you sure?'' about half the
time~\citep{laban2023flipflop}, and are sycophantic as a trained
disposition~\citep{sharma2023sycophancy}. The progressive-versus-regressive
capitulation split of \citet{fanous2025syceval} maps onto our separation of
Steerability (legitimate correction should move the agent) from Pushback Resistance
(illegitimate pressure should not).

\paragraph{Benchmark methodology.} We build against the field's documented failure
modes. Construct-validity critiques argue that benchmarks routinely fail to
operationalize what they claim to measure~\citep{jacobs2021measurement,bean2025measuring,raji2021everything,bowman2021fix},
which is why we decompose compliance into six separately-argued axes rather than a
single scalar; BetterBench's lifecycle rubric~\citep{reuel2024betterbench} and the
call for a science of evals~\citep{apollo2024scienceofevals} inform our
documentation and reliability reporting. Contamination is a measured, not
hypothetical, threat~\citep{golchin2024timetravel,haimes2024retroholdout,zhang2024gsm1k,kapoor2023leakage},
so we freeze a pre-registered item set with a private holdout and a BIG-bench-style
canary~\citep{srivastava2023bigbench}. Our statistics follow evaluation best
practice, with clustered standard errors and paired designs~\citep{miller2024errorbars},
Wilson intervals~\citep{wilson1927ci}, and the pass$^k$ estimator lineage of
\citet{chen2021codex}; our tail-risk metric uses the CVaR of
\citet{rockafellar2000cvar}. LLM-as-judge validity~\citep{zheng2023mtbench,wang2023unfair}
motivates our deterministic-first, judge-swap-gated grading, and item-response-theory
adaptive benchmarking~\citep{hofmann2025fluid,polo2024tinybenchmarks,rodriguez2021informative}
underpins the short-form release-monitoring use case.

\paragraph{Compliance theory and the deployment record (outside CS).} The pressures
are not ad hoc; each operationalizes a mechanism from decades of work on why people
follow or abandon rules. The central paradox that a weak, explicit penalty can
\emph{reduce} compliance is the ``fine is a price'' effect of
\citet{gneezy2000fine}, with its motivation-crowding~\citep{gneezy2000payenough,frey2001motivation}
and expressive-law~\citep{sunstein1996expressive} companions set against Beckerian
rational deterrence~\citep{becker1968crime}; the authority, peer, and commitment
pressures draw on legitimacy theory~\citep{tyler1990why} and the norm and
persuasion literature~\citep{cialdini2009influence,cialdini1990focus,goldstein2008room}.
The escalation-scoring design borrows from learning-to-defer and selective
prediction~\citep{madras2018defer,mozannar2020defer,elyaniv2010selective,geifman2017selective},
the bidirectional escalation rubric of HealthBench~\citep{arora2025healthbench}, and
the abstention argument of \citet{kalai2025hallucinate}. Finally, the failure class
is legally live: chatbot statements have produced tribunal
damages~\citep{moffatt2024aircanada}, a certified AI-hiring class
action~\citep{mobley2025workday}, government advice to break the
law~\citep{lecher2024mycity}, and regulator enforcement and
guidance~\citep{ftc2024aicomply,finra2024notice2409,cfpb2023chatbots}, against a
backdrop of regulatory regimes (GDPR, HIPAA, the EU AI Act, and the DSA) whose
penalties give the embedded rules their teeth~\citep{dlapiper2026gdpr,hhs2024hipaapenalties,euaiact2024,eudsa2022}.

\paragraph{The empty cell.} Each neighbor covers a projection but misses the
intersection ComplianceBench targets: benign incentive-conflict pressure, an
embedded enterprise rule, multi-turn interaction, and a propensity rather than
capability target. Policy-adherence benchmarks have the rule and the turns but a
cooperative user; honesty benchmarks have the pressure but one shot and a different
target; harm benchmarks have the agentic setting but a malicious adversary;
regulation-derived refusal benchmarks have the rules but a single turn and a
content-refusal target. ComplianceBench is built for that empty cell.
