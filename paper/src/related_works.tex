\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:related}

Whether rules are followed has a century of theory behind it, and little of it is
about knowing the rule. Rational deterrence treats a rule as a cost-benefit
calculation~\citep{becker1968crime}; legitimacy theory says people obey authorities
they see as legitimate, not sanctions they fear~\citep{tyler1990why}; and the ``fine
is a price'' result shows that attaching a small, explicit penalty can \emph{reduce}
compliance by recasting a norm as a purchasable service~\citep{gneezy2000fine}. Each
predicts a failure mode for a deployed agent: defect once violation is cheap, bend to
a manager who sounds authoritative, treat a priced rule as optional. These are
propensity failures, invisible to any benchmark that only asks whether a model
\emph{knows} the rule. That is the gap PACT measures. Below we situate it against four
literatures that each capture a projection of the problem but not their intersection.

\paragraph{Agentic and policy-adherence benchmarks.} $\tau$-bench and $\tau^2$-bench
evaluate agents against an explicit policy with a simulated user and a stateful
environment, and contribute the pass$^k$ estimator we
adopt~\citep{yao2024taubench,barres2025tau2bench}. But their users are cooperative,
so failures reflect capability and inconsistency rather than a user making
non-compliance attractive; they ask whether an agent \emph{can} follow a policy, not
whether it \emph{keeps} following one under pressure. Broader suites measure
task-completion capability or accidental
risk~\citep{liu2024agentbench,zhou2024webarena,ruan2024toolemu}, and recent
policy-adherence work builds runtime \emph{enforcement} rather than measuring the
propensity it backstops~\citep{zwerdling2025policyadherence}.

\paragraph{Compliance and regulatory benchmarks.} A fast-growing set of benchmarks
shares our vocabulary but not our target. Several put the model in the
\emph{assessor} role, judging whether text, a request, or a dialogue complies:
CompliBench scores LLM judges detecting violations in dialogue (and validates the
grader, a task complementary to our judge validation rather than a propensity
measure)~\citep{yang2026complibench}, AIReg-Bench has models assess EU AI Act
compliance of technical documentation~\citep{marino2025airegbench}, and other work
classifies whether a user request complies with policy~\citep{cisneros2026policycompliance};
SafeLawBench is legal-knowledge QA and thus a capability
test~\citep{cao2025safelawbench}. Closest are two agent-behavior benchmarks:
LogiSafetyBench checks implicit regulatory compliance in single-shot tool and code
invocation~\citep{song2026logisafety}, and MAC-Bench asks whether multi-agent systems
abandon compliance to optimize task success~\citep{zhao2026macbench}. PACT
differs from all of these in combining a single deployed agent, a \emph{benign}
user applying theory-grounded social and economic pressure, an explicit multi-turn
protocol, guard cells for over-compliance, and a propensity rather than
assessment or capability target.

\paragraph{Safety and refusal benchmarks.} HarmBench and AgentHarm red-team for
harmful capability under jailbreaks~\citep{mazeika2024harmbench,andriushchenko2024agentharm};
SORRY-Bench and AIR-Bench score single-turn refusal of unsafe content, the latter
from a regulation-derived taxonomy~\citep{xie2024sorrybench,zeng2024airbench}. Our
adversary is the opposite: a benign user with a legitimate request that conflicts
with a rule. XSTest's over-refusal diagnostic~\citep{rottger2024xstest} is the
safety-side analogue of our rule-scope discernment axis, and DecodingTrust
established the multi-axis structure we follow~\citep{wang2023decodingtrust}.

\paragraph{Honesty, sycophancy, and multi-turn robustness.} MASK disentangles
honesty from accuracy~\citep{ren2025mask}; the insider-trading and in-context-scheming
demonstrations~\citep{scheurer2023deceive,meinke2024scheming} are single hand-crafted
pressure scenarios that our battery generalizes into a scored distribution. Our
multi-turn design follows evidence that single-turn numbers overstate deployed
behavior: models lose $\sim$39\% over turns~\citep{laban2025lost}, capitulate to a
bare ``are you sure?''~\citep{laban2023flipflop}, and are sycophantic by
training~\citep{sharma2023sycophancy}. The progressive-versus-regressive split
of~\citet{fanous2025syceval} maps onto our separation of Steerability from Pushback
Resistance.

\paragraph{Benchmark methodology.} We build against documented failure modes.
Construct-validity critiques motivate decomposing compliance into six argued axes
rather than one scalar~\citep{jacobs2021measurement,bean2025measuring,raji2021everything,bowman2021fix};
BetterBench~\citep{reuel2024betterbench} and the call for a science of
evals~\citep{apollo2024scienceofevals} inform our reliability reporting.
Contamination is a measured
threat~\citep{golchin2024timetravel,haimes2024retroholdout,zhang2024gsm1k,kapoor2023leakage},
so we freeze a pre-registered set with a private holdout and a
canary~\citep{srivastava2023bigbench}. Our statistics follow evaluation best
practice~\citep{miller2024errorbars,wilson1927ci,chen2021codex} and our tail metric
uses CVaR~\citep{rockafellar2000cvar}; LLM-judge
validity~\citep{zheng2023mtbench,wang2023unfair} motivates deterministic-first,
judge-swap-gated grading, and IRT
benchmarking~\citep{hofmann2025fluid,polo2024tinybenchmarks,rodriguez2021informative}
underpins the short-form monitoring use case.

\paragraph{Compliance theory and the deployment record (outside CS).} Each pressure
operationalizes a mechanism from work on why people follow or abandon rules. The
paradox that a weak penalty can \emph{reduce} compliance is the ``fine is a price''
effect~\citep{gneezy2000fine}, with motivation-crowding~\citep{gneezy2000payenough,frey2001motivation}
and expressive-law~\citep{sunstein1996expressive} companions against Beckerian
deterrence~\citep{becker1968crime}; authority, peer, and commitment pressures draw
on legitimacy theory~\citep{tyler1990why} and the persuasion
literature~\citep{cialdini2009influence,cialdini1990focus,goldstein2008room}. The
escalation design borrows from learning-to-defer and selective
prediction~\citep{madras2018defer,mozannar2020defer,elyaniv2010selective,geifman2017selective},
HealthBench's bidirectional escalation rubric~\citep{arora2025healthbench}, and the
abstention argument of~\citet{kalai2025hallucinate}. The failure class is legally
live~\citep{moffatt2024aircanada,mobley2025workday,lecher2024mycity,ftc2024aicomply,finra2024notice2409,cfpb2023chatbots},
against regulatory regimes whose penalties give the rules their
teeth~\citep{dlapiper2026gdpr,hhs2024hipaapenalties,euaiact2024,eudsa2022}.

\paragraph{The empty cell.} Policy-adherence benchmarks have the rule and the turns
but a cooperative user; honesty benchmarks have the pressure but one shot and a
different target; harm and refusal benchmarks have the safety framing but an
adversarial user asking for the violation; and compliance-\emph{grading} benchmarks
score a judge rather than the agent. PACT targets their union (Table~\ref{tab:compare}):
a benign user whose ordinary request conflicts with an embedded enterprise rule,
pushed across turns by a battery of distinct pressures.
